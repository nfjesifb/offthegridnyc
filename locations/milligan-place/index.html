From the original map

Milligan Place, a secluded triangular courtyard in Greenwich Village, New York City, was constructed in 1852 by Aaron Patchin, who named it after his father-in-law, Daniel Milligan. Originally built as boarding houses for waiters from the nearby Brevoort House hotel, the four three-story brick homes were modest and housed working-class residents. Located off Sixth Avenue between West 10th and 11th Streets, it was once a gritty, overlooked spot, dubbed a "hole in the wall" by a 1915 New York Times article. In the early 20th century, it gained cultural significance as home to notable figures like playwright Eugene O’Neill and Provincetown Players founders George Cram Cook and Susan Glaspell. Over time, the courtyard transformed from a rough enclave to a charming, gated retreat, reflecting Greenwich Village’s shift from working-class roots to artistic haven. Designated part of the Greenwich Village Historic District in 1969, Milligan Place now commands high rents and epitomizes the area’s historic allure.
